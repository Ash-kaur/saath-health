1. Executive summary

Saath Health is a vernacular, subscription-based care platform for people with diabetes and hypertension in Tier-2 and Tier-3 India. The plan asks for ₹30 crore in two rounds (₹6 crore seed, ₹24 crore Series A). In the base case the business reaches EBITDA breakeven in month 40 and ₹137.5 crore of revenue in year 5.

A member pays about ₹450 a month for a health coach who speaks their language, doctor consults, a subsidised BP and glucose kit, home diagnostics and generic medicines delivered monthly. Employers and insurers pay a lower fee for the same programme.

2. Problem and market opportunity

India has the world's largest pool of people who need continuous chronic care but get episodic, expensive, mostly English-language treatment. Diabetes and hypertension need monthly monitoring, adherence support and periodic tests, which an occasional OPD visit does not deliver, and specialists are scarce outside metros.

The serviceable market is the paying slice of the 8.64 crore on treatment who live outside the top metros, can afford about ₹450 a month and own a smartphone. The plan reaches 235,370 paid members by year 5, about 0.3% of the people already on treatment, so it does not depend on market growth.

3. Solution and product

Saath is a monthly care loop, not a one-off consultation: the member measures, the coach nudges, the doctor adjusts, the pharmacy delivers, and the next reading shows whether it worked.

PlanPrice per monthWhat it includes
Coaching₹299Vernacular health coaching and monitoring
Standard₹499Coaching plus doctor consults and the subsidised device kit
Family₹899Standard plan for a household
Employer / insurer programme₹300 per memberSame programme with fewer coach touches, paid by the employer or insurer

The three consumer plans are blended at about ₹450 a month in the model. Languages at launch: Hindi, Punjabi and English.

5. Go-to-market and competition

Launch in the Chandigarh tricity, Punjab and Haryana, where trust is built through local pharmacies, clinics and community health camps rather than ads alone, then expand city by city.

The edge has to come from depth in one region and one care loop: local-language coaching, one human coach per 300 members, and pharmacy partnerships in smaller towns. Large platforms can copy features, so retention (churn below 4% a month by year 3) is what must be proven.

6. Operations, clinical governance and regulation

AreaApproach
Clinical protocolsA clinical lead writes treatment-escalation rules; the AI only flags readings and drafts messages; a registered doctor makes every prescribing decision
DoctorsEmpanelled tele-consult doctors paid per consult (₹300 in the model), under India's telemedicine practice guidelines
Pharmacy and labsLicensed pharmacy partners and accredited partner labs, so inventory and lab capex stay off the balance sheet
DataHealth data is sensitive; consent-first collection and alignment with India's digital health data rules; legal review before launch
TeamFixed opex starts at ₹14 lakh a month (about 12 people across product, clinical, operations and finance) and reaches ₹1.2 crore a month by year 5

Regulatory points here are the author's understanding, not legal advice. Confirm telemedicine, e-pharmacy and data-protection requirements with a healthcare lawyer.

11. Risks, milestones and what to validate

RiskWhy it mattersMitigation
Churn stays above 5% a monthYear-1 LTV/CAC is already only 1.7x; the downside case adds 25% to churnCoach-led onboarding, family plans, annual prepay option, track 90-day retention first
CAC higher than ₹2,000Marketing and kits are 40%+ of cost in years 1-3Pharmacy and camp channels, referral credits, cut the kit subsidy if payback exceeds 12 months
Clinical safety or liabilityA missed escalation harms a patient and the brandDoctor-led protocols, AI limited to flagging, audit of escalations, appropriate insurance
Data privacy and regulationSensitive health data, telemedicine and e-pharmacy rulesConsent-first design, legal review before launch
Large platforms copy the modelFunded competitors already serve chronic careRegional depth, vernacular coaching, retention data as the moat
Employer and insurer sales are slowB2B is only 7% of year-5 revenue, so the plan survives without itTreat B2B as upside until two paying pilots are signed

Milestones to earn the Series A by month 12:

  • 8,700 paid members across the pilot towns
  • Monthly churn trending toward 5%
  • Marketing CAC at or below ₹2,000 and payback near 10 months
  • Two employer or insurer pilots in discussion

Before this goes to investors, validate with real customers the ₹450 blended price, the 55% medicine attach rate, the 300 members per coach ratio and the 6% year-1 churn, then replace those inputs and re-run the model.
